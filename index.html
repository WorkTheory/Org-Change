WorkTheory Differentiator

Change that works for the business — and for the people in it.

Most change programs focus on what needs to change. WorkTheory asks a harder question: what will it feel like to live through this change? Employee experience isn't a soft consideration — it's a leading indicator of whether change will stick. We design every stage of our change engagements with EX as an explicit lens, not an afterthought.

Change is rarely the problem. Unstructured change is. When organisations move fast without a disciplined approach to people, culture, and alignment, transformation programs stall — not because the strategy is wrong, but because the conditions for change were never properly designed.

WorkTheory's change model brings together McKinsey 7S to diagnose what's misaligned, Kotter's 8-Step Model to structure and lead the response, and an employee experience thread woven through every stage — ensuring change is designed well and felt well.

Before designing change, we need to understand what's actually misaligned. Diagnostic Foundation

McKinsey 7S — Understanding what's misaligned

Strategy

Is your people strategy aligned to business direction? Where is the gap?

EX impact: low alignment creates confusion and disengagement

Structure

Reporting lines, layers, spans of control. How the organisation is divided and coordinated.

EX impact: unclear structure creates role ambiguity and frustration

Systems

Processes, workflows and technology. How work actually gets done day to day.

EX impact: broken systems erode trust and daily experience

Shared Values

The beliefs and culture at the centre of the organisation. The foundation everything rests on.

EX impact: values misalignment is the #1 driver of attrition

Style

Leadership approach and how leaders behave. What they model, reward and tolerate.

EX impact: leadership style is the single greatest EX variable

Staff

People — their capabilities, roles, and how they're developed, motivated and retained.

EX impact: investment in staff is felt immediately and directly

Skills

Core competencies of the organisation — what it does well and what it needs to build.

EX impact: capability gaps create anxiety and reduce confidence

Change Leadership Model

Kotter's 8 Steps — Leading change with discipline

Phase 1 — Create the climate for change
1

Create Urgency

Build a compelling case for why change is necessary now.

EX: employees need to understand the why before they can engage with the how
2

Build the Guiding Coalition

Assemble a team with authority, credibility and energy to lead change.

EX: coalition must include voices that employees trust
Phase 2 — Engage and enable the organisation
3

Form a Strategic Vision

Define what the future looks like. Make it simple, credible, and inspiring.

EX: vision must answer "what does this mean for me?"
4

Enlist a Volunteer Army

Move beyond the coalition — engage broadly so change becomes a shared movement.

EX: this is where employee experience of change is most shaped
5

Enable Action by Removing Barriers

Identify and dismantle structural, cultural and process obstacles.

EX: unremoved barriers signal to employees that change isn't real
6

Generate Short-Term Wins

Design early visible wins to build confidence and maintain momentum.

EX: wins make change feel achievable — critical for morale
Phase 3 — Implement and sustain
7

Sustain Acceleration

Use early wins to drive deeper change across systems, structures and behaviours.

EX: pace and communication must stay human — fatigue is real
8

Institute the Change

Embed new behaviours in culture, leadership and systems so change sticks.

EX: change is only sustained when it becomes the new normal for people
